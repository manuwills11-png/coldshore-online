# ColdShore Online: "Salt & Signal"

Co-op for 1 to 6 players. You run a crew out of a shared settlement, take missions into the county, bring back supplies, upgrade the settlement and survive the raids that come for it. The story is separate from the single-player campaign: a different crew, a different threat, and the same county.

## The story

Three weeks after the quarantine, a voice comes over every radio in the county. It calls itself WARDEN. It sends your crew on jobs and watches how you do them. Your crew sets up in the empty warehouses of Harbor Row, which you name **Haven**, and takes the work.

The jobs get stranger. WARDEN is breeding things, steering hordes with sound, and testing which groups hold together. The last operation of the first arc goes up the lighthouse to find out who has been talking to you.

**Arc 2: Undertow (build 57).** The tape in the lamp room was your own voice. Someone else was listening: **MERCY**, a voice on the hospital ship *St. Agnes* offering a cure, and **Captain Rook** of Meridian Recovery, paid to burn whatever Halden left behind. A downed Meridian pilot, **Okafor**, tells you the truth: MERCY is Halden's second program. WARDEN tested which groups hold together; MERCY tests what they will do for a cure. The arc ends aboard the *St. Agnes*, broadcasting everything to every radio in the county. Haven's radio operator **Quill** talks you through the operations.

## Operations (builds 56 and 57)

There are no random contracts any more. Online is **twenty-four hand-built operations** in eight chapters and two story arcs. Each one is designed around something the squad has to do together. Finished operations stay on the board, so you can replay them on Hard or Nightmare for supplies, and every few operations Haven itself comes under siege.

The design follows two references. **Left 4 Dead** was tuned so that it can't be beaten without cooperation, and its specials punish players who wander off. **The Last of Us** paces each chapter as setup, a new place, a new problem, strain, then relief. Every operation brings in one new co-op mechanic, then later operations combine them.

### Co-op mechanics

| Mechanic | How it works | Solo |
|---|---|---|
| **Cover** | A cutting or prying job only progresses while no zombie is within reach of the work point. Two working together go a bit faster. | Clear the area, then work. |
| **Two keys** | Switches 300+ units apart must each have someone on them holding E at the same moment. Progress drains if anyone lets go. Waves come at the switches. | One switch. |
| **Carry** | Pick up the objective with E (E again to put it down). The carrier can't shoot or sprint and moves at 55% speed, or 82% with a teammate beside them. If the carrier goes down, it drops. Runners come for it. | Slow and exposed. |
| **Flare** | Hold E at the flare to light it. The horde walks to it for 40 to 50 seconds. When it burns out, they turn round and come for you. | Light it, loot, relight it. |
| **Barricades** | Hold E at each marker to build a wall. Zombies have to go round. Brutes smash through fast, and crowds wear walls down. | Same. |
| **Protect** | A machine with health that part of every wave goes for. If it dies, the operation fails. | Same. |
| **Quiet** | During a quiet approach, a screamer going off brings a horde. | Same. |
| **Grabbers** | A hunched special that lunges and pins one player. The pinned player can't move or shoot and takes damage until it's killed. | Mash E seven times to throw it off. |
| **Relay** (57) | Boxes in a chain, each woken by holding E for about two seconds. After each one, the next has to be woken inside the window or the chain drops back to box one. | 30% longer window. |
| **Escort** (57) | The vehicle crawls along a route only while a player is within 8 m of it and fewer than two zombies stand in front of it. It has health, and part of every wave goes for it. | Same; clear the front, then walk it. |
| **Fuel** (57) | One can at a time to the generator; the next can appears somewhere else. Waves grow with each can. | 4 cans instead of 6 to 8. |
| **Gas** (57) | Inside the spore cloud, anyone more than 10 m from the scrubber takes 6 damage a second. The bubble moves with the scrubber, carried or on the ground. | Carry it yourself. |
| **Hunt** (57) | The Matriarch has two or three phases. At the end of each it bursts (acid), sings (calls the dead and spawns more) and runs to its next lair. While running it can't be hurt. | Same. |
| **Defuse** (57) | Several charges, each needing about 9 seconds of E with no zombie within 7 m. One clock for all of them; when it runs out, the operation fails. | 25% more time. |
| **Zones** (57) | Rings 20+ m apart (or Haven's barricades) that each need someone standing in them; the timer only runs while every ring is held. | One ring. |
| **Evac** (57) | A helicopter or boat in a ring, on a timer. When everyone standing is inside, you leave. If the timer runs out with some of you aboard, the rest are left behind and the bonus is lost; with nobody aboard, the operation fails. | Same. |
| **Spitters** (57) | Keep their distance and lob acid that pools on the ground for about 7 seconds. | Same. |

Every operation ends with getting everyone home. A downed player is picked up by a teammate holding E. If the whole squad is down at once, the operation fails.

**Bonus objective:** every operation rolls one bonus: nobody goes down, finish inside 9 minutes, kill an elite, 60 kills between you, or nobody bleeds out. Meeting it pays 50% more.

## Free choice: who goes where

There are no rules about who goes where. Every player picks for themselves.

- **Deploy** on any operation takes only you. Anyone else can **join** your squad whenever they like, from the board or from the map, or start a different operation of their own.
- Every open operation and every squad already out is marked on the map (M), at its town. Click one to see it, then press Enter to deploy, J to join or W to set a waypoint.
- Clicking anywhere else on the map sets a waypoint: a diamond on screen with the distance, a beam in the world and a dot on the minimap. Right-click or X clears it.
- Each squad has its own sim host, its own zombies and its own objectives. Players at Haven see each other walking around.
- Pulling out of an operation with others still in it leaves the squad running.

## Playing together

- **Humvee: driver and gunner.** Online, one player drives the Humvee and another mans the roof gun. Walk up to a Humvee a teammate is driving and press **E** to climb onto the turret (Shift+E takes the gun on a parked one). The gunner aims with the mouse and holds fire. The gun overheats if held too long, and the driver can't fire while someone is on it. Offline, you drive and shoot yourself (hold fire or F).
- **Riding along.** Press **E** next to any car a teammate is driving to ride in it. Passengers can shoot their own weapon out of the window. E gets you out.
- **Player colours (build 58).** Every online player gets a colour slot: their arrow on the map and minimap, their name tag, their pins and pings, a tint on their clothes, an armband and a backpack. If two players picked the same face, the second is shown with a different one, so nobody looks alike.
- **Pings.** Press **P** (or click the middle mouse button) to mark where you're looking. Your squad sees a beam in your colour, your name and the distance for 14 seconds.
- **Pins (build 58).** Click anywhere on the map (M) to drop a pin. It stays until you remove it (click it again, right-click, or X), survives a reload, and your crew sees it as "NAME'S PIN" in your colour on their map, minimap, compass and screen. The big map has its own frame (title and legend above, help below): drag to move, wheel to zoom, Z for the whole region.
- **GPS (build 58).** The minimap draws the way to your pin, or to the objective when you have no pin, along the roads (A* on a 10 m grid over the whole region; water and buildings are blocked, roads are cheap). Under the minimap: distance by route, and "turn left / turn right / keep straight" with the compass direction. It re-routes when you leave the line.
- **Backpack (build 58).** TAB opens it: every weapon (rarity, mag and spare ammo), items with their quick keys, ammo by calibre, crafting, and Perks. Six quick-select slots: hover a gun and press 1 to 6 to put it on that key; empty slots fill themselves with your other guns.
- **Giving (build 58).** Walk up to a teammate and press **E** (or full stop): the backpack opens in give mode. Pick a weapon, an item or a calibre, set how many, and Give. The server checks you are both there and close, then confirms; only then is it gone from your bag, and if it says no, it goes straight back. A gun you hand over leaves your armory and joins theirs. It works the same in both directions.
- Players already **revive** each other (hold E on a downed teammate).

## Netcode

**Build 63: one shared world, no visible lag.**
- Every client learns the server's clock from round trips (it trusts the fastest ones). Every position is stamped with the moment it was true, so each screen moves teammates and their cars forward to where they are now instead of drawing them in the past. State goes out 30 times a second while moving (10 when still), with velocity. Measured with two clients over a simulated 70 to 110 ms connection: a driving teammate shows up about 50 ms behind (mean 1 m off at 25 m/s while weaving) and one on foot about 35 ms (0.2 m).
- A slow connection skips a zombie update instead of queueing it, so it can never delay player positions.
- Every car has an id from where it was made, and the same spot always holds the same model and colour. A car somebody drives is the same car (model, colour, fuel, already hotwired) on everyone's screen; if a client doesn't have it, it makes it.
- The county is built from fixed seeds per stage (and three.js object ids no longer draw from the seeded random stream), so trees, cars, houses and loot spots match between players. Time of day (a 24-minute day) and weather come from the server clock.

- Teammates are drawn slightly in the past (about 100 to 350 ms, adapting to how jittery the connection is), between two real updates, so they move smoothly. They face the way they walk, play a reversed walk when backing up, and face their aim when shooting.
- Every state message carries the sender's clock, so packets that arrive bunched together are still spaced out correctly.
- The sim host streams only zombies that moved or changed, every 0.1 s near players and at least once a second for the rest (about a third of the old traffic). Puppets keep moving at their last speed between updates.
- Teammates' cars are smoothed the same way.
- Online, the game never pauses: opening the map, a menu or switching tabs keeps your game (and the zombies you host) running. Slow frames are caught up in small steps instead of slowing the game down.

## The settlement: Haven

Haven is a real place on the map: a walled yard on the open ground east of the bay. Every operation starts at the gate and ends back inside it. Between operations you can walk around it with the rest of the crew.

Everything you see comes from the building levels saved on the server, so it changes as the crew builds:

- **Walls:** a scrap barricade, then a timber palisade, a walkway, concrete blocks, steel plate and razor wire.
- **Watchtowers:** up to five, with searchlights from level 3.
- **Housing:** tents, then cabins (2 more per level). Settlers salvage scrap while you are offline.
- **Farm:** crop fields and scarecrows, then a chicken coop, a pig pen, a cow pasture, a greenhouse and a windmill. The animals wander, peck and graze.
- **Power:** a generator with an exhaust plume, solar arrays, a wind turbine from level 3, floodlights and string lights over the plaza.
- **Workshop, Infirmary, Garage and Radio mast:** each grows a size with its level.

**The gate (build 61).** Haven's gate opens as anyone walks or drives up to it and swings shut behind them. Shut, it is a wall the dead can't get through; it stays open while a squad is out on an operation.

**The car park (build 61).** The garage and its painted apron are the crew's car park. Get out of a vehicle inside it and the server keeps it there for the whole crew, between sessions (colour, fuel and damage included). Drive it out and the space frees up. There are 6 spaces plus 2 per garage level.

### Build 65: smoother frames

These numbers were measured in the browser at HIGH quality, in a town street.
- **Frozen matrices.** three.js recomputed the world matrix of all ~40,000 objects every frame, which took about 6.6 ms. Anything that has held still for two checks is now frozen and skipped until it moves, which brings that down to about 1.5 ms.
  - Cars, opened crates and kicked props thaw themselves when they move.
  - Characters, Haven, door leaves and anything with a light or sprite are never frozen.
  - A rolling check thaws, for good, anything that moves without asking. None turned up in an operation or a two-player session.
- **Cars at a distance.** A line that hid the first-person car body was switching every car back on each frame, so all 139 cars were drawn at any distance. The far cut-off works now. The real-model cars (Humvee, S-10, Crown Vic, GLS, BMW, Challenger, Lamborghini) are batched into one mesh per material with a far version, like the other cars: 18 to 53 meshes each became 7 to 16.
- **Draw calls.** Down from about 1,550 to about 1,050 a frame at HIGH:
  - The static county is merged into 120 m chunks instead of 60 m.
  - Flat things on the ground (roads, kerbs, floors, rugs) no longer cast shadows they can't show.
  - Moonlight shadows skip furniture inside houses, indoor loot and wall lanterns, which the roof already shadows. The flashlight still shadows them.
  - Town props are instanced per 400 m cell.
- **Shadows on slower machines.** When frames take longer than 18 ms on foot, the shadow maps redraw every other frame instead of the game dropping to MEDIUM. At normal frame rates, and always while driving, they redraw every frame.
- **Memory.** Merged static geometry stores normals and vertex colours as bytes. The geometry waiting in memory to be drawn fell from 785 MB to 473 MB, and the same saving applies on the GPU.
- **Online check.** Re-tested with two clients over a simulated 70 to 110 ms connection:
  - The world is identical on both clients: cars, trees, loot, walls, props, furniture and the Hartwell house.
  - A teammate's rendered car is 55 ms behind on screen (1.1 m on average while weaving at 25 m/s), and a teammate on foot 35 ms.
  - Riding along, giving weapons and operation 1 all work.

## Difficulty

Each mission has a **threat** level that sets zombie numbers, how many are runners, how often specials appear, and how hard they hit.

`threat = 1 + chapter step + 0.45 × (players − 1) + 0.04 × operations done (max 1.5) + tier bonus`, where the chapter step is 0.7 per chapter up to chapter 4 (2.1), then 0.45 per chapter after that (3.9 at chapter 8)

Tier bonus is 0 for Normal, +1.5 for Hard and +3 for Nightmare. Harder tiers pay out more.

On top of threat:

- **Elites** (armoured, red-eyed, 4.5× health) join infestations, clears and the last quarter of every hold.
- The last quarter of a hold brings 40% bigger waves and extra brutes.
- From threat 2, runners come out of the trees behind the squad on the way home.

- Threat 1 to 3: mostly shamblers.
- Threat 3 to 6: runners, screamers and night missions appear.
- Threat 6 and up: bloaters, brutes, bigger waves and road events on every drive.

Failing a mission costs a share of the supplies carried, never the settlement.

## Rewards

Each player earns XP and kills for their own profile. The crew earns supplies, chapter progress and new board missions. Rewards scale with threat and tier.
